# Ideas backlog (approved, not built)

Visual direction: Swiss, but not empty. Every visual carries information.

## Approved
- **Giant number per screen.** Today: next dose time (`13:00`). History: `94%`. Med detail: `22 left`.
- **History as a poster.** Full-bleed month grid, month name set huge (`OCTOBER`).
- **Rare colour blocks.** Solid orange slab behind the next dose; full dark screen for "That's everything." at night.
- **Instrument motion.** "Now" line moving down Today, course bar filling, countdown ticking. Things move only because time moved.
- **Store graphics as posters.** One huge line of type, real UI cropped below.

## Reference brief (user, reviewed)
- **01 Architectural imagery** — yes, limited to launch, completion and one rare editorial panel. Bundled duotone photos (ink on paper), cropped hard, no text over busy areas. Never wallpaper.
- **02 Living daily rhythm** — yes. Countdown, "now" line, past rows dimming and course bar as one connected system.
- **03 Tactile feedback** — yes. Check draws in 140 ms, row settles to grey, orange moves to the next dose, one haptic click, undo for 4 s.
- **04 Material character** — yes to two paper tones and shared-element slides between screens. No shadows, no fake paper texture.
- **05 Meaningful completion** — yes. "That's everything." with a calm summary (`5 OF 5 · LAST AT 22:04`), no score.
- **06 Night atmosphere** — yes. Designed, not inverted: warmer paper-dark, secondary text softer, orange as the only light source.

## Earlier agreed
- Field notes on the all-done screen (`FIELD_NOTES.md`).
- Course end screen: "Course complete." + `DELETE {MED}?`
- Copy tone: plain, factual, quietly on your side. No exclamation marks, streaks or confetti.
- Feature shortlist: pills left with refill date, Meds cabinet page, plain monthly summary.
- Polish list: splash and first run, haptics with undo, reminder reliability (first three).

## Not picked
- Pill shape icons per med (left out for now).
- Blog or medication fun facts (offline promise, health-claim risk).
